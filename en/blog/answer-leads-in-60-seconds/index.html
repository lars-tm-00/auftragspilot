Answer a lead in 60 seconds: why speed wins jobs

Friday, 7:40 pm. Someone fills in your form and asks for a quote. You see it on Monday. They signed with a competitor on Saturday.

The problem isn't the number of leads

Many businesses spend thousands on ads and get enough enquiries. They get lost afterwards: the enquiry lands in an inbox, someone calls back when there's time and reaches nobody. Evenings, weekends and holidays, nobody answers at all.

What waiting really costs

The study by Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington (Harvard Business Review, 2011) measured the response times of 2,241 US companies. On average the first reply took 42 hours, and 23% never replied. A sub-study with 1.25 million leads showed: firms that responded within an hour were seven times as likely to have a qualifying conversation as firms that responded an hour later. Compared with waiting 24 hours, the factor was over 60.

Do the maths for your business: if just ten out of 100 enquiries a month are lost to slow replies, how much revenue is that at your average job value?

The fix: answer before the lead clicks away

No person can answer every enquiry within a minute, especially not at night. An assistant on WhatsApp can. At Auftragspilot it works like this:

  1. The enquiry comes from the ad, the form or WhatsApp.

  3. Two or three questions check whether they fit: need, timing, budget.
  4. The AI offers genuinely free slots from your calendar and books directly.
  5. If they don't reply, the assistant follows up politely.
